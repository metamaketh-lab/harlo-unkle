Power Trip in HDB: Causes and What to Do

A safety-first guide to repeated power trips in Singapore HDBs — what to check before resetting, when it’s an emergency, and why EMA licensing matters.

A power trip is your home’s warning system, not just an inconvenience. In HDBs, where circuits are shared across rooms and ageing MCBs are common, repeated trips can point to overloads, moisture, bad appliances, or deteriorating wiring. Here’s what to do — and what not to do — when the breaker shuts off.

Common HDB Power Trip Causes

  • Overloaded circuit: Multiple high-draw appliances on the same 13A socket — aircon, dryer, induction hob, and a water heater together can trip a standard HDB circuit.
  • Ageing MCB or RCBO: Older BTO flats and resale HDBs still have original breakers that weaken over time.
  • Moisture ingress: Bathroom leaks, balcony seepage, or A/C condensation near switches can cause earth leakage trips.
  • Faulty appliance: Space heaters, kettles, and cheap third-party chargers are frequent trip culprits.
  • DIY or unapproved wiring: Hidden extensions, spliced connections, or old-school copper replacements done without an EMA electrician.

What to Do in the First 10 Minutes

  1. Switch off all appliances on that circuit.
  2. Reset the MCB once. If it holds, reconnect devices one by one to find the offender.
  3. If it trips again immediately, leave it off and remove the highest-draw appliance.
  4. Do not reset a breaker that won’t stay on — this is a signal to stop, not a challenge to overcome.

When to Call an Electrician

Call if the trip recurs, if you smell burning near the DB box, see discoloured sockets, or notice warm faceplates. For HDB electrical work, choose an EMA licensed electrician. That licence isn’t just paperwork — it means the technician is insured, inspected, and accountable under Singapore’s electrical safety framework.

EMA Licensing and HDB Rules

EMA requires licensed electrical workers for installation, alteration, and repair work. HDB enforces this through contractor vetting and can require re-inspection after unauthorised work. If you’re renovating or replacing wiring, insist on seeing the electrician’s licence before work starts.
