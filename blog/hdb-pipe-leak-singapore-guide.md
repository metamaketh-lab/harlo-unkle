# HDB Pipe Leak Singapore: Complete Guide — Causes, Fixes, and When to Call a Pro

You hear that drip. Drip. Drip. coming from your kitchen cabinet at 11pm. Or you notice a faint damp patch spreading slowly across your bedroom ceiling that was definitely not there last week. For most Singapore HDB dwellers, a pipe leak is not a maybe — it's a *when*.

And let's be real: your first instinct is probably to grab a wrench and YouTube it. That's fine — until the leak is coming from a pipe inside your wall, or your downstairs neighbour taps on your door to tell you their ceiling is dripping.

**This guide covers everything you need to know about HDB pipe leaks in Singapore** — from the first warning signs and DIY checks to PUB regulations, realistic repair costs for 2026, and the situations where calling a licensed plumber is genuinely the only sane option.

## Why HDB Pipe Leaks Are a Different Beast

Singapore's public housing model means most of us live in high-density blocks built to standardised specifications. That's actually a double-edged sword for pipe leaks.

On the positive side, HDB flats share common plumbing layouts. A plumber who's worked in Clementi knows what to expect in Clementi — the pipe runs, the riser locations, the standard fitting sizes. That familiarity keeps quotes predictable and repairs faster.

On the downside, many of our flats are reaching, or have already reached, the point where original plumbing materials are showing their age. HDB has been running upgrading programmes — particularly the [Home Improvement Programme (HIP)](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/residential/living-in-an-hdb-flat/improving-your-flat) — but not every block has been done. If you're living in a pre-2000 flat in Ang Mo Kio, Tampines, or Woodlands, your pipes could be forty-odd years of original galvanised steel or early PVC.

Galvanised steel pipes rust from the inside out. You won't see the corrosion until the leak happens. PVC pipes become brittle under UV exposure and thermal cycling. Neither is catastrophic, but both mean pipe leaks in HDB follow a predictable lifecycle — and knowing where you are in that cycle helps you budget and prepare.

## The Most Common Causes of HDB Pipe Leaks

Understanding *why* your pipe leaked is just as important as fixing the immediate problem. Here are the usual suspects in Singapore HDB flats.

### Corrosion and Age

Galvanised steel pipes, common in older HDB flats before PVC became the norm, corrode internally. The rust builds up, narrowing the pipe bore until flow becomes restricted and pressure finds a weak point. These leaks are often slow, seepage-type leaks that run behind walls or under floors for weeks before showing visible signs.

### Poor Connections and Workmanship

The simplest cause is also the most common: a connection that wasn't tightened properly, or a soldered joint that cracked as pipes expanded and contracted under hot water cycling. This is especially common in DIY plumbing jobs that were done without a PUB-licensed plumber.

### High Water Pressure

Singapore's water pressure is regulated, but it varies across the network. Flats at the bottom of a 20-storey block may experience higher static pressure than those at the top. Over time, sustained high pressure stresses pipe joints and valves. Pressure surges from rapid valve closures — the toilet cistern filling, the washing machine solenoid cutting off — create micro-fatigue in pipe walls.

### Physical Damage

Moving furniture and drilling walls for installations are everyday activities in HDB living. A nail or wall plug driven through a concealed pipe will give you a leak instantly. So will a heavy cabinet shifted across a floor that crushes a pipe running under floorboards. Before you drill or shift anything substantial in a pre-2000 flat, it's worth knowing where your water lines run.

### Freezing and Thermal Stress

This is less relevant in Singapore's climate, but thermal cycling from hot water pipes is real. Your kitchen sink line sees hot water, then cools, then hot again. PVC and even copper expand and contract. Over years, this stresses joints and couplings.

### Tree Root Intrusion (Rare but Real)

For HDB ground-floor units or blocks with garden areas, invasive tree roots can find their way into drainage pipes through joints or cracks. It's uncommon in high-rise flats but shows up occasionally in older blocks with poor ground drainage — particularly in estate areas like Punggol and Woodlands where greenery was established alongside the buildings.

## Warning Signs: Don't Ignore These

A pipe leak rarely announces itself politely. Here's what to watch for:

**Unexplained water bill increases.** This is the silent indicator. If your PUB water bill jumps by 30% or more and your household usage hasn't changed, you almost certainly have a leak. PUB's domestic water charge for 2026 averages around SGD $2.00–$2.80 per cubic metre, but hidden leaks running for months can push your annual bill up by hundreds.

**Damp patches or discolouration on walls and ceilings.** Brownish-yellow staining, peeling paint, or wallpaper that won't stay down usually points to water movement — either from above (your leak is affecting a neighbour) or from within (a wall pipe is seeping).

**Mould or mildew growth.** Bathrooms, kitchens, and corners of rooms that stay damp despite cleaning are classic signs of ongoing moisture. In Singapore's humid climate, mould appears within days of persistent dampness.

**Reduced water pressure or flow.** If your shower suddenly feels like a drizzle or your kitchen tap takes forever to fill a pot, there might be pressure loss from a leak somewhere in the system.

**Hissing or running water sounds.** Turn off all taps and appliances, then listen. If you hear water moving where there shouldn't be any, trace it. Santa rosa — or whatever your block's pipe riser id — doesn't wait for the weekend.

**Musty odours.** Stagnant water behind walls or under floors develops a distinct odour. If your kitchen or bathroom suddenly smells off despite cleaning, investigate.

## What to Do the Second You Find a Leak

Panic is not helpful. What is helpful is a five-step action plan.

**1. Stop the water supply.** Locate your flat's stop tap — usually in the kitchen or near the bathroom. Turn it off. If the leak is severe or you can't reach the stop tap, you may need to shut off the building's main valve (ask your town council or look for the landing valve).

**2. Contain the water.** Mop, bucket, towel — do whatever you need to protect your flooring and, crucially, your downstairs neighbour's ceiling. If water is spreading fast, place containers strategically and consider moving electrical appliances away from the area.

**3. Call your neighbour below.** If water is visibly dripping through the ceiling, tell them immediately. Not only is this neighbourly, but it also starts the documentation you'll need if the situation escalates. A quick message on the estate WhatsApp group works.

**4. Document everything.** Photos, videos, notes on what happened and when. This matters for insurance and any disputes later.

**5. Decide: DIY or professional.** The rest of this guide helps you make that call.

## The First 15 Minutes: Before the Plumber Arrives

If you've called in a professional — which we'll help you decide is the right call below — there are things you can do while waiting:

- Relocate valuables and electronics away from the leak area.
- Place bowls or towels under dripping points to limit water damage.
- For toilet leaks, you can use a small cup and bucket to manually empty the bowl — this prevents overflow while waiting. Take the lid off the cistern and check if the fill valve is stuck open; if it is, try gently wiggling the float arm.
- For kitchen or bathroom cabinet leaks, move food, cleaning products, and paper goods out of reach.
- Turn off your water heater — water-contact with electrical elements can be dangerous.

## DIY Checks You Can Safely Do Yourself

Before calling anyone, run through these simple checks. They take ten minutes and could save you a service call.

**Check visible pipe connections.** Under every sink, at every toilet connection, at your washing machine's inlet hoses — look for condensation, drips, or mineral deposits. Tighten visible fittings with a spanner. Do not overtighten — PVC threads strip easily.

**Test your toilet for a silent leak.** This is the food-colouring trick your grandmother told you, and it still works. Put a few drops of food colouring (or a leak detection tablet) in the cistern. leave it for 20 minutes without flushing. If colour appears in the bowl, your flapper valve is not sealing properly. A replacement flapper costs a few dollars and is easy to swap.

**Inspect your stop tap and isolation valves.** Make sure they actually turn. In many HDB flats, these valves seize from disuse. You want yours working before an emergency.

**Look under the floor.** If you have access to the service panel or sub-floor in your HDB flat (some older flats have removable panels near the toilet), check for dampness on the underside of floorboards.

**Monitor your water meter.** Turn off all taps and water-using appliances. Go to your water meter. If the dial is still moving, water is flowing somewhere that it shouldn't be. This confirms a leak exists even if you can't see it.

None of these require tools you can't buy at the neighbourhood hardware store. If nothing shows up but your water bill keeps climbing, the leak is probably hidden — and that's when you need a pro.

## PUB Rules: What Singapore Law Actually Requires

Plumbing in Singapore is regulated by PUB, the national water agency. It's worth understanding what this means for your HDB pipe leak.

**Licensed plumbers.** Any plumber carrying out work on public water supply pipes in Singapore must hold a valid PUB Waterworks Plumber Licence. There is no grey area here — it's the law under the Public Utilities Act.

**What work requires a licensed plumber?**
- Any repair or modification to the public water supply system
- Pipe relaying or replacement that connects to public supply
- Water meter installations or relocations
- Work on the common drainage stack (the vertical pipe serving multiple floors)

For a minor tap washer replacement inside your own flat on a downstream pipe, the PUB licence requirement is technically less strict. But it's still smart to use a licensed plumber because:
- Their insurance covers damage
- PUB can provide a referral if something goes wrong
- [Harlo Unkle's plumbing service](/plumbing.html) verifies licences for every pro listed

**What if you hire a non-licensed plumber for regulated work?** PUB can prosecute, and penalties are meaningful. For homeowners, using a licensed plumber also protects you from coverage disputes if the work fails and damages your flat or your neighbour's.

**Leak insurance and neighbour disputes.** If your pipe leaks and damages your downstairs neighbour's property, you are legally liable — regardless of whether you knew about the leak. Home insurance policies vary, but most require that any plumbing work done on the property be by licensed contractors for claims to be valid.

## Leak Detection: How Pros Find Hidden Leaks

Not all leaks are obvious. A pipe running through a kitchen wall cavity could be losing litres per hour with no external sign until the damage is already done. Professional leak detection methods include:

**Visual inspection.** The starting point. The plumber checks all obvious connections, examines walls for dampness, looks for minor corrosion, and assesses the overall system.

**Acoustic leak detection.** Experienced plumbers use sensitive microphones and listening equipment to hear water escaping from pipes inside walls or under floors. The sound of pressurised water escaping through a crack is distinctive — like a very quiet hiss — and can be pinpointed with practice.

**Infrared thermography.** Thermal imaging cameras detect temperature differences in walls. A wet area inside a wall will show up as a cooler (or warmer, depending on the water temperature) patch compared to the dry surrounding material. This is particularly useful for large flat areas where the leak source is uncertain.

**Pressure testing.** The plumber isolates a section of pipe and pressurises it with air or water, then watches the gauge. If pressure drops, there's a leak in that section. This method narrows down the location.

**Pipe relaying and endoscopic cameras.** In the worst-case scenario, a plumber may need to cut into walls or floors to access pipes directly. DIY-influenced homeowners sometimes hesitate here — but delaying access to a confirmed hidden leak just increases the damage and cost.

## What Leak Repairs Actually Cost in 2026

Pricing in Singapore varies by job complexity, accessibility, and whether the job requires wall hacking. Here's the realistic range as of 2026.

### Simple Spot Repair

**SGD $150 – $400**

The pipe has a small crack, visible leak, or loose joint that's accessible. The plumber shuts the water, replaces a short section of pipe or fittings, re-pressurises, and tests. Usually done in under an hour. Pricing depends on whether you need new fittings or if a simple coupling patch works.

### Leak Detection Only

**SGD $100 – $300**

If you just can't find the leak and want a professional to locate it, many plumbers offer standalone detection. This usually covers the time and equipment but not the actual repair, which is quoted separately once the leak is found.

### Hidden Leak with Access Work

**SGD $600 – $2,000+**

This is the scary one. The leak is behind a wall, under a floor, or in a ceiling void. The plumber needs to gain access — which means opening up, finding the leak, repairing it, and then patching things back up. You're paying for demolition, repair, and restoration. Costs climb fast if the affected area is large, if it's in a hard-to-reach location like a bathroom riser, or if the plumber discovers additional corrosion requiring more pipe work than initially expected.

### Full Pipe Relaying (Section)

**SGD $800 – $2,500+**

Not just a patch. The plumber replaces a significant run of piping — perhaps the entire cold water feed to your kitchen, or the feed to your ensuite bathroom. This takes longer, requires more materials, and may involve routing through multiple rooms or ceilings.

### Complete Plumbing Riser Replacement

**SGD $2,500 – $5,000+**

If the building's vertical riser (the main supply pipe running up through the block) is affected — common in older HDB blocks — the job is substantial. Town councils and HDB occasionally do these upgrades under HIP or maintenance programmes. For individual owners in older blocks, you may need to coordinate with HDB. This is the kind of expenditure that justifies good home insurance.

### Emergency Leak Response (After Hours)

**SGD $300 – $800+ (call-out)**

Late nights, weekends, public holidays. If you have an active leak that can't wait, the call-out fee is higher. Many plumbers apply a 30-50% surcharge for non-standard hours. The repair cost is then added on top.

### PUB Licence Fee

**SGD $50 – $150 (if applicable)**

For work that requires a licensed plumber on the public supply system, PUB charges an administrative fee. Your plumber should include this in their quote or break it out so you know it's real.

## When to DIY vs When to Call a Licensed Plumber

We've said it before and we'll say it again: the line between a fine weekend project and an expensive, neighbour-damaging mistake is thinner in HDB plumbing than in almost any other home repair.

**Do try yourself:**
- Replacing a tap washer or cartridge
- Swapping a showerhead or hose
- Replacing a toilet flapper or fill valve
- Tightening a visible pipe connection
- Changing a visible section of flexible tap connector (braided hose)

**Definitely call a pro:**
- Any leak behind walls, under floors, or in ceilings
- Pipe relaying or section replacement inside walls
- Anything connected to the main water supply and public distribution system
- Leaks affecting your downstairs neighbour
- Gas line anything (different licence entirely)
- If you've already tried a DIY fix and the leak persists or worsened

If you're in a 30-year-old flat in Toa Payoh or Bedok and your kitchen wall is showing dampness, don't open it yourself. Call a licensed plumber. They'll know whether to open from your side or the neighbour's side, and they'll do it in a way that minimises subsequent repair work.

## How to Find a Good HDB Plumber in Singapore

Not all plumbers are built for HDB flats. Here's how to pick one who won't waste your time or money.

**Check the PUB licence.** Ask for the licence number. Verify it online at PUB's licensed plumber registry. This takes five minutes and is non-negotiable for regulated work.

**Get a written quote before work starts.** Verbal estimates aren't binding. A quote that breaks down labour, materials, and any licence fees protects both you and the plumber.

**Confirm what's included.** Does the quote include wall patching, paint touch-up, and disposal of old materials? Or is that extra? Find out before the plumber shows up with a hole in your wall and a surprised look.

**Experience with HDB specifically.** Ask whether they've done similar work in your estate or block type. A plumber who works on landed houses may not know HDB pipe layouts — and that slows them down.

**Reviews and referrals.** Ask in your estate's resident WhatsApp or Telegram group. Someone three floors up almost certainly had a leak repaired last year. Real recommendations from real neighbours beat Google reviews (which can be gamed).

**Insurance.** Make sure the plumber has public liability insurance. If they accidentally burst a pipe that floods your unit and the one below, you want coverage.

## Preventing Future HDB Pipe Leaks

Prevention is just scheduled maintenance with better PR.

**Know your flat's plumbing age.** If you're in a block built before 2000, consider having a plumber do a full visual inspection of accessible pipes. It'll cost you SGD $150–$300 and can catch corroding joints before they leak.

**Monitor your water bill monthly.** PUB bills are now available in near-real-time through their app or online portal. A consistent upward trend over two or three billing cycles, with no change in your behaviour, means investigate.

**Don't overtighten fittings.** It feels counterintuitive, but tightening a tap connector or isolation valve until your knuckles turn white can crack the fitting or the pipe behind it. "Snug plus a quarter turn" is the rule. Use a spanner sized correctly for the fitting.

**Replace flexible connector hoses every five years.** Those braided metal hoses under your sinks and toilets degrade internally. They're cheap to replace (SGD $15–$40 each) and way cheaper than water damage.

**Be careful with drilling.** Before putting up shelves or mounting TVs in older flats, use a stud finder or pipe detector. The five seconds this takes is worth far more than the cost of drilling through a concealed water line.

**Keep isolation valves accessible.** Don't bury kitchen or bathroom cabinet isolation valves behind cleaning products and storage. If a pipe bursts at 2am, you want to reach the stop tap without playing furniture Tetris in your pyjamas.

## What Waterproofing Has to Do With It

Pipe leaks and waterproofing failures are often connected. A bathroom pipe leak can compromise the waterproofing membrane under your tiles — and a waterproofing failure can make a small pipe leak into a big structural problem.

If the leak has gone on for a while — dampness, ceiling stains, mould — you may need both pipe repair and waterproofing remediation. This is where jobs expand in scope and cost. Having a plumber assess the full situation (not just the pipe they can see) avoids surprise bills later.

## Partnering With HDB and Town Councils

Sometimes a pipe leak is not just your problem — it's the block's problem.

HDB towns consult with town councils and building management for maintenance of common property plumbing. If you suspect that the leak originates from a building riser or common pipe stack rather than your internal plumbing, report it to your town council. They'll investigate and coordinate repair.

For flats enrolled in HDB's upgrading programmes (HIP, [Home Improvement Programme II](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/residential/living-in-an-hdb-flat/improving-your-flat/hip)), water supply pipe replacement is sometimes included. Check your eligibility and programme status at the HDB Hub or online.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I know if my HDB pipe is leaking or just sweating?**
Pipe "sweating" (condensation on the outside) happens when cold water runs through pipes in humid air — common in Singapore. condensation drips are usually minor and come and go. A steady drip, damp spreading from a pipe, or a rising water bill points to an actual leak somewhere. When in doubt, monitor your water meter with all water off.

**Can PUB come and fix a pipe leak in my HDB flat?**
PUB does not do residential repairs. PUB's role is to supply clean water and enforce regulations. For repairs inside your flat, you need a licensed plumber. For suspected leaks on the public supply or common riser, report to your town council.

**How much does leak detection cost in Singapore?**
Standalone leak detection typically costs SGD $100–$300. This is time and equipment only. The actual repair is extra. Some plumbers waive the detection fee entirely if you proceed with the repair through them.

**Will HDB cover the cost of a burst pipe in my flat?**
HDB covers common property and building infrastructure. Internal flat plumbing is the homeowner's responsibility. If a burst in your flat damages a neighbour's unit, your home insurance should cover their claim — provided the work was done by a licensed contractor (if required).

**My water bill jumped suddenly. How long does it take a plumber to find a hidden leak?**
It depends. Simple accessible leaks are found quickly. Hidden leaks requiring access work or detection equipment can take a few hours to a full day. Ask the plumber for an estimated detection timeline when you call — a good plumber will give you a rough timeframe before they start.

**Is epoxy grouting enough to fix a pipe leak?**
Epoxy grouting seals tile grout lines and prevents water from seeping through bathroom floors and walls. It does not fix a leaking pipe. If you have a pipe leak *and* compromised grout, you need both: pipe repair first, then grouting or epoxy injection for the waterproofing layer.
