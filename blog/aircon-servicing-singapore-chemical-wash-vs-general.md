# Aircon Servicing Singapore: Chemical Wash vs General — What You Actually Need

If you've lived in Singapore for more than a week, you know the deal. You come home, flip on the aircon, and instead of that beautiful cold rush, you get... lukewarm air and a faint musty smell. Something's wrong.

Now you're faced with the eternal question every Singapore homeowner dreads: **do I need a general servicing or a chemical wash?** And the follow-up nobody asks but should: **how do I know I'm not getting scammed?**

Let's break this down properly.

## The Basics: What Each Service Actually Does

### General Aircon Servicing

This is your routine maintenance — think of it like an oil change for your car. A general service typically includes:

- **Filter cleaning** — dust and particles get trapped in your filters over time, reducing airflow and cooling efficiency.
- **Evaporator coil cleaning** — the indoor unit's coils accumulate grime that affects heat exchange.
- **Condenser coil cleaning** — the outdoor unit's coils get dirty from exposure to Singapore's humidity and air pollution.
- **Drainage tray and pipe flushing** — prevents water from backing up and dripping into your room (a common complaint).
- **Fan and blower check** — ensures the fan is running at the right speed without unusual noise.
- **Gas level check** — a technician will verify refrigerant levels, though they won't top up during a standard service.
- **Basic electrical inspection** — checking wiring connections and thermostat operation.

A general service takes roughly 30-60 minutes per unit. It's quick, relatively affordable, and should be done **every 3-4 months** for a typical Singapore household.

**Cost in 2026: SGD $40 – $80 per unit (general service)**

Most aircon companies offer discounts for servicing multiple units at once. A 3-unit package typically runs SGD $100 – $180 total. If someone's quoting you more than SGD $80 per unit for a general service, ask what's included — you might be getting a better deal than you think, or you might be overpaying.

### Chemical Wash (Chemical Overhaul)

A chemical wash is the deep clean. It's more intensive, takes longer, and costs more — but sometimes it's exactly what your system needs. Here's what it involves:

- **Full dismantling** of the indoor unit — the front cover, evaporator coil, drainage tray, fan blower, and filters are all removed.
- **Chemical cleaning** of each component — a specialised cleaning solution (usually an alkaline-based chemical) is applied to dissolve stubborn buildup that regular water cleaning can't shift.
- **High-pressure rinsing** — components are thoroughly rinsed to remove chemical residue and dissolved grime.
- **Reassembly and testing** — everything goes back together, and the technician checks cooling performance, drainage, and airflow.

A chemical wash takes 1-2 hours per unit. The chemicals used are stronger than what's safe for general servicing, which is why this should only be done by trained technicians.

**Cost in 2026: SGD $120 – $250 per unit (chemical wash/overhaul)**

Again, multi-unit discounts are common. A 3-unit chemical wash package typically costs SGD $300 – $600.

## So Which One Do You Actually Need?

Here's the honest answer most aircon companies won't give you directly: **most homes only need a general service every 3-4 months.** A chemical wash is not routine maintenance — it's a periodic deep clean.

### You Need a General Service When:

- Your aircon is working fine but you want to keep it that way (preventive maintenance)
- You're getting your scheduled 3-4 month service
- The aircon is cooling properly but airflow has reduced slightly
- There's minor dust buildup but no bad smell

### You Need a Chemical Wash When:

- **Persistent musty or sour smell** that doesn't go away after a general service. This usually means mould or bacteria growth on the coils or drainage tray.
- **Water leaking** from the indoor unit after general servicing has been done. This often means the drainage pipe is clogged internally and needs chemical treatment.
- **Cooling has dropped significantly** despite regular servicing. Heavy grime on the evaporator coils can insulate them and reduce heat exchange dramatically.
- **It's been over a year** since the last chemical wash, even with regular general servicing. Some buildup simply can't be removed without dismantling and chemical treatment.
- **You've moved into a new-to-you property** and the previous owner's aircon maintenance history is unknown.

### The Red Flag: Every-Time Chemical Wash

If an aircon technician arrives for a scheduled general service and immediately tells you that you need a chemical wash — **every single time** — that's a pricing strategy, not a diagnosis.

A genuine chemical wash need is obvious: visible grime, persistent odour, water issues, or significantly degraded performance. If your aircon is cooling well and smells fine, you don't need chemicals today. Full stop.

## Common Aircon Scams in Singapore (And How to Spot Them)

Unfortunately, the aircon servicing industry in Singapore has its share of dodgy operators. Here are the most common tricks:

### 1. The Upsell on Every Visit

**The play:** Technician arrives for a $50 general service, then tells you the coils need a chemical wash, the gas needs topping up, and the fan motor is "almost dead."

**The reality:** Some of these things might genuinely need attention. But a good technician will explain what they're seeing and let you decide — not pressure you into adding $300 to the bill.

**Protect yourself:** Ask for evidence. If the coils are dirty, ask to see them. If gas is low, ask them to show you the pressure gauge reading. Any legitimate technician will be happy to walk you through it.

### 2. Inflated Chemical Wash Pricing

**The play:** Quote SGD $200+ per unit for a chemical wash when the market rate is SGD $120-$180.

**The reality:** Some companies charge premium rates for "specialist chemicals" or "imported solutions." While there are genuine quality differences between cleaning chemicals, they don't justify a 2x markup on the service.

**Protect yourself:** Get at least two quotes before agreeing to any chemical wash. If you're in a condo where your management has an approved vendor list, check those first — they're often competitively priced.

### 3. Fake Gas Top-Up Charges

**The play:** Tell you the refrigerant gas is low and charge SGD $50-$150 for a "top up."

**The reality:** A properly sealed system shouldn't need gas top-ups for years. If your technician says you need gas topped up every service, there's a leak somewhere — and just topping up without fixing the leak is like pumping air into a tyre with a nail in it. The real fix is leak detection and repair.

**Protect yourself:** If gas is being topped up regularly, insist on a leak test. A good technician will identify and fix the leak rather than just topping up repeatedly.

### 4. The "Cannot Be Fixed" Diagnosis

**The play:** Declare your aircon unit dead and recommend a full replacement (from their supplier, naturally).

**The reality:** Sometimes units genuinely do reach end of life. But many "dead" aircon issues are fixable — a faulty capacitor, a stuck fan motor, or a tripped circuit breaker can all look catastrophic to an untrained eye.

**Protect yourself:** Always get a second opinion before replacing an entire unit. A SGD $50 diagnostic from a different company could save you thousands.

## How Often Should You Service Your Aircon in Singapore?

Singapore's climate is harsh on air conditioning systems. The combination of heat, humidity, and constant use means your units work harder than in most countries. Here's a practical schedule:

- **General servicing:** Every 3-4 months (quarterly)
- **Chemical wash:** Every 12-18 months, or sooner if problems develop
- **Full system check (including outdoor unit):** Annually
- **Gas check:** As part of annual service, or if cooling drops unexpectedly

If you run your aircon 8+ hours daily (like most of us do), lean toward the more frequent end of these ranges.

## Tips for Getting the Most Out of Your Aircon

A few things that make a real difference between services:

- **Clean your filters monthly.** Yes, monthly. Singapore's air quality means filters clog faster than you'd expect. Most HDB and condo filters can be popped out, washed with water, and dried in 10 minutes.
- **Set the temperature to 24-25°C.** Colder isn't always better. Running at 18°C just makes the compressor work harder and drives up your electricity bill. Twenty-four degrees is comfortable and efficient.
- **Keep the outdoor unit ventilated.** Don't store things in front of or on top of your condenser. It needs airflow to function properly.
- **Use the timer function.** Running the aircon from 11pm to 6am (8 hours) is enough for most bedrooms. Constant overnight running wastes electricity and wears the system faster.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does aircon servicing cost in Singapore?**
General servicing typically costs SGD $40-$80 per unit, while a chemical wash runs SGD $120-$250 per unit. Multi-unit packages offer better value. Prices vary by company, district, and whether it's a peak or off-peak booking.

**How often should I service my aircon in Singapore?**
Every 3-4 months for general servicing is the standard recommendation. Given Singapore's humid climate and heavy usage, quarterly maintenance keeps your system running efficiently and prevents costly breakdowns.

**Is a chemical wash necessary every time?**
No. A chemical wash is a periodic deep clean, not routine maintenance. Most homes need one every 12-18 months. If someone recommends a chemical wash at every visit, get a second opinion.

**Why does my aircon smell after servicing?**
If the smell persists after a general service, you likely need a chemical wash. Mould and bacteria on the evaporator coils or drainage tray cause odours that surface cleaning can't fully address.

**Should I buy an aircon servicing package?**
Annual packages can save money if you're committed to regular maintenance. Compare the per-service cost against ad-hoc bookings. Just make sure the package includes all the services you need — some strip packages exclude gas checks or outdoor unit cleaning.

**Can I service my aircon myself?**
You can clean filters and wipe down the indoor unit cover yourself. Anything involving the coils, drainage system, refrigerant, or electrical components should be left to trained technicians. Improper handling can void your warranty and create safety hazards.
